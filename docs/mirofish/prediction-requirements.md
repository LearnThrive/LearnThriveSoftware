# Prediction requirements for MiroFish

How to use: upload `seed-site.md` (in this folder) as the seed material, then paste **one** of the three requirements below into the prediction box. Run them as three separate simulations; they ask different questions of the same site.

Treat the result as a way to find objections and questions you had not thought of, not as research. The people in it are language-model characters. Before changing the site because of something it says, check it with a handful of real parents or tutors.

Suggested settings: start small. The project warns that runs use a lot of model quota, so keep to fewer than 40 rounds and the smallest number of agents the interface allows, put a spending cap on the model key, and look at the first report before scaling up. Do not upload anything else from this repository (in particular `.env` files, `apps/web/LEGAL_REVIEW.md`, the completion report or the safeguarding agreement text, which is still a draft).

## 1. Parents: first impressions and trust

> LearnThrive Tuition is a small UK online tuition service offering one-to-one lessons from Year 1 to A-Level and 11+ preparation. The attached text is what a visitor sees on its website; the site publishes no prices for families. Simulate how parents and carers of children aged 6 to 18 in the UK, talking in parent groups and school-gate forums, react when a friend shares this site. Include a mix: parents of primary-age children, 11+ candidates, GCSE and A-Level students, and children with additional needs; some price-led, some anxious about online safety, some who have used tutors before. Predict which statements build trust and which raise doubt (especially the safeguarding and DBS claims, "40+ students supported", the one-to-one promise and the lesson reports), what they would ask before sending an enquiry, what would make them leave or choose a competitor, and which kinds of parent are most and least likely to enquire. End with a ranked list of the five changes to the site most likely to increase good enquiries.

## 2. Prospective tutors: would the right people apply?

> LearnThrive Tuition is recruiting self-employed online tutors in the UK: £10 an hour per student, hours that depend on student availability, no formal training, an Enhanced DBS check including the Children's Barred List arranged and paid for by the company unless the tutor already holds a suitable certificate, and a firm safeguarding agreement that every applicant must read and agree to, then reconfirm every year. The attached careers pages are what an applicant sees; the agreement text itself is deliberately not included, so do not invent its contents. Simulate how university students, recent graduates, working teachers and career changers react when they find this role on tutor job boards and student forums. Predict who applies and who drops out, the main objections (pay, no training, unpredictable hours, how strict the agreement sounds), what they assume about how reliable the work is, and how a negative thread could spread. End with a ranked list of the five changes to the careers pages most likely to attract good applicants without weakening safeguarding.

## 3. Sceptics: which claims would be challenged?

> The attached text is the public website of LearnThrive Tuition, a small UK online tuition service that is also recruiting tutors. Simulate a sceptical review by a safeguarding professional, a consumer-rights blogger, a tutor from a competing agency and a parent who has been disappointed by a tutoring company. Each reads every page looking for claims that cannot be checked, statements that could mislead, gaps, inconsistencies between pages, and anything that sounds more reassuring than it is (for example around DBS checks, "never in groups", progress, lesson reports, and how concerns are handled). Predict what each would publish or say, which points would spread furthest, and which are fair. End with a list of the claims most worth rewording or evidencing, in order of risk.
