Our story

#### Why we started LearnThrive

LearnThrive Tuition began with a simple belief: every student deserves support that fits them — whatever their starting point, their learning ability, or their background.

Founders — and childhood friends —Abdurrahman Mustafa andTahasin Hasan set out to make great tuition genuinely accessible: the kind that adapts to how each student learns, rather than expecting every child to learn the same way. That belief still drives everything we do.

Meet the founders

#### The people behind LearnThrive

##### Abdurrahman Mustafa

Co-founder

I'm Abdurrahman Mustafa, co-founder of LearnThrive Tuition. Ever since I was young, I despised the idea of tutoring and dreaded attending sessions every Sunday. Then, at 17, I became the very thing I'd dreaded — and realised that teaching and helping people who are struggling to progress is one of the most beautiful things in life.

Over the years, as I questioned what I wanted to do and how I wanted to build my future, my childhood friend Tahasin and I decided to start our own tutoring service. At LearnThrive, you'll do more than raise your grades — you'll grow, and learn to love the very subject you're studying.

##### Tahasin Hasan

Co-founder

Hi, I'm Tahasin Hasan — and yes, Hasan is actually my surname, not me introducing myself twice 😂. I started tutoring at 16, carried it through A-levels, survived university while still tutoring, and somehow ended up making mock exams for students while stressing over my own exams too.

Over the years, I realised that most students don't struggle because they "can't do it" — sometimes they just need it explained in a way that actually makes sense to them. Now, as co-founder of LearnThrive Tuition, I get to take everything I've learned from tutoring, studying and many questionable sleep schedules, and use it to make education more personal, enjoyable and effective.

Does LearnThrive support learners with special educational needs?

How are lessons adapted for learners with additional needs?

Lessons can be adjusted in a number of ways, including pacing, the use of visual aids and breaking tasks into smaller, manageable steps. The aim is to present material in a way that works for the individual learner and supports their progress.

## Safeguarding page

Safeguarding

### Safeguarding information

Children’s welfare comes first. This page explains who is responsible for safeguarding, what every tutor must do before teaching, how to raise a concern and the safety considerations that matter around online tuition.

Last reviewed 9 October 2026
Written for the services and features available on this website.

#### Scope

This information is for parents, guardians, students, tutors and anyone concerned about a child’s safety. The public website provides information and an enquiry route; it is not an online classroom, private messaging service or lesson-recording system.

#### A commitment to student welfare

LearnThrive Tuition is committed to treating concerns about a child’s safety or wellbeing seriously and acting in line with applicable law and safeguarding guidance. Children should be treated with respect, listened to and supported to raise concerns.

Safeguarding concerns may involve abuse, neglect, bullying, exploitation, inappropriate behaviour or online harm. No adult working with a child should inflict physical or psychological harm.

#### Who is responsible

LearnThrive’s safeguarding lead is Tahasin Hasan and itsdeputy safeguarding lead is Abdurrahman Mustafa. Every tutor must report any safeguarding concern to the safeguarding lead, or to the deputy if the lead cannot be reached.

You can reach them on Tahasin Hasan: [phone number] Abdurrahman Mustafa: [phone number] or [email address].

#### Tutor checks and the safeguarding agreement

No tutor teaches any student, or has any contact with one, until every check is complete.

Every tutor must:

- pass identity checks and right-to-work checks;

- agree to an Enhanced DBS check including the Children’s Barred List, arranged by LearnThrive. LearnThrive pays for it unless the tutor already holds a suitable certificate or chooses to pay for their own check;

- read and agree to the safeguarding agreement as part of applying, and agree to the same expectations in the tutor agreement they sign; and

- reconfirm the safeguarding agreement every year, and whenever a new version is issued.

Read the safeguarding agreement. It helps LearnThrive to:

- make safe recruitment decisions;

- protect children and adults at risk from harm, and minimise threats to staff, clients, assets, data and reputation; and

- meet its legal and regulatory obligations.

#### Safe online tuition

Appropriate online tuition arrangements should include:

- professional language, behaviour and boundaries;

- age-appropriate communication focused on learning;

- a suitable learning environment and safe use of devices;

- clear information for parents and guardians about how lessons and contact take place; and

- a way for a child or adult to say when something feels unsafe.

LearnThrive lessons are not recorded by default. Before lessons begin, parents and guardians should discuss the platform, contact arrangements, supervision and any recording arrangements with LearnThrive.

#### Parents and guardians

A parent or guardian should normally make the initial tuition enquiry. Use the general form for a brief description of learning support and avoid unnecessary sensitive or medical information. It is not an emergency reporting service.

Take an interest in your child’s online learning, help them use devices safely and encourage them to tell a trusted adult if something worries them. Ask questions about safeguarding arrangements before deciding whether tuition is suitable.

#### Raising a concern with LearnThrive

If a child is in immediate danger, call 999 first. For a concern connected with LearnThrive that is not an immediate emergency, a parent, a student, a tutor or anyone else can contact the safeguarding lead as soon as possible:

- email [email address], making clear that your message concerns safeguarding; or

- call Tahasin Hasan: [phone number] Abdurrahman Mustafa: [phone number].

Give enough information to explain the concern and how you can be contacted. LearnThrive will take the concern seriously and consider appropriate action, including referral to relevant safeguarding services where necessary.

A concern should be recorded factually, and safeguarding records should be kept separately from routine learner records, with restricted access.

Information should be handled sensitively, but complete confidentiality cannot be promised when sharing is needed to protect a child or meet a legal obligation.

#### Urgent and independent help

- If a child is in immediate danger, call 999.

- For other concerns about a child, contact the children’s social care team at their local council. GOV.UK explains how toreport child abuse.

- Adults can contact the NSPCC helpline on0808 800 5000.

- Children and young people can contact Childline free on0800 1111.

Do not wait for a response from LearnThrive if urgent help is needed. You do not need our permission to contact an independent or statutory safeguarding service.

#### Further guidance

Parents and guardians can read the Department for Education’sguidance on choosing tuition and other out-of-school activities. Safeguarding guidance for providers includesWorking Together to Safeguard Children in England and theNSPCC’s guidance for tutors.

You must be 18 or over, have the right to work in the UK (including for self-employed work), and be confident in the subject you want to teach.

We teach online and one to one, in Maths, English and Science from Year 3 to A Level, and in 11+ preparation.

What is the pay, and what are the hours?

Pay is £10 an hour per student. Hours depend on student availability. Tutors are self-employed.

No formal training is currently provided. Opportunities to progress may exist later; they are still being worked out, so we cannot promise anything yet.

How are applications assessed?

We look at your CV and your experience, and at your results across GCSE, A Level and any degree (undergraduate or higher), all together. A strong later result is taken into account even if earlier results were weaker. We do not ask for a fixed number of references.

How do I apply?

Use the online application: one page, in eight numbered sections. Part of it is reading and agreeing to the safeguarding agreement. You can also apply by email at [email address]; if you do, we will send you the safeguarding agreement to read and confirm before anything else happens.

The application does not save what you type, so have your CV and details ready. If you leave the page before sending, your answers are lost.
Go to the application

What should I include?

Your CV is required. A cover letter is optional: upload a file, paste it into the box, or both. You also answer a few short questions, give your results at GCSE, A Level and degree level (at least one), and tell us your availability. You do not need to give referee details.

Which files can I upload, and how big can they be?

Your CV and your cover letter file can each be a PDF or Word (.docx) file of up to 3 MB. Supporting documents can be PDF, Word (.docx), JPG or PNG files: up to 5 files, each up to 3 MB. All your files together must be 12 MB or less.

We cannot accept older Word (.doc) files, files that can contain macros, compressed archives, or PDFs that are protected with a password or contain scripts. If a file is refused, use Save as PDF and try again. Please do not send bank details or health information.

Do I have to upload identity and right-to-work documents?

No. In the supporting documents step you can upload them now, until we put a separate secure process in place, or you can provide them later when we ask. Uploading them now is optional.

They are used to verify your identity and right to work, and are handled by the hiring team. If you work with LearnThrive they are kept while you do and for 12 months after, then deleted, so you do not have to start again if you return. If your application is unsuccessful they are deleted 6 months after the decision. Please do not upload a DBS certificate.

What about the DBS check, and who pays?

Every tutor must agree to an Enhanced DBS check including the Children’s Barred List. LearnThrive arranges it and pays for it, unless you already hold a suitable certificate or choose to pay for your own check. Any start is subject to the outcome.

A suitable existing certificate is an Enhanced DBS certificate that includes the Children’s Barred List for the children’s workforce, and that is subscribed to the DBS Update Service, so that LearnThrive can check its status online with your permission. LearnThrive decides whether to accept it. Anything else needs a new check, arranged and paid for by LearnThrive, or paid for by you if you choose.

The application asks only whether you hold a certificate and whether you use the Update Service. Please do not upload a certificate or send any DBS information through the form.

What is the safeguarding agreement?

It sets out what every tutor must do, and must never do, when working with children. Every applicant must read and agree to it as part of applying: you tick each part, answer six short questions about it (this is not training), and type your full name. The same expectations then form part of the tutor agreement you sign. Tutors reconfirm it every year, and whenever a new version is issued.

Read the safeguarding agreement before you apply.

Do you ask for references?

There is no fixed number of references. We look at your CV, your experience and your results at every level, all together.

What happens after I apply?

We will reply within 5 working days. The hiring steps are:

- Fill in the application, including reading and agreeing to the safeguarding agreement.

- The team reviews your CV, your experience and your results.

- We get in touch about next steps.

- We check who you are and that you can work in the UK.

- An Enhanced DBS check including the Children’s Barred List, arranged by LearnThrive.

- It includes the safeguarding expectations you agreed to when you applied.

- Once every check is complete. No tutor teaches any student before then.

How long do you keep my information?

If your application is unsuccessful, we delete it up to 6 months after the decision; if you ticked the box asking us to keep you in mind for future roles, up to 12 months. Identity documents are always deleted 6 months after the decision, whichever you choose.

If you become a tutor, identity and right-to-work documents are kept while you work with LearnThrive and for 12 months after. Agreement and reconfirmation records are kept for 6 years after, and longer where linked to a safeguarding concern.

The applicant privacy notice has the full detail.

How do I withdraw my application, or ask about my information?

Email [email address] and quote the reference in your confirmation email. You can ask us to withdraw your application, to delete your information or to tell you what we hold.
